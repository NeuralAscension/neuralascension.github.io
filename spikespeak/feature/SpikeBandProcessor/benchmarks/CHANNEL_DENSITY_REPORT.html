SciChart channel-density benchmark — how many traces can the live view show?#

Correction (2026-07-12): an earlier version of this report accidentally measured empty surfaces (a buildColumns() bug blanked the traces during the sweep — now fixed). The numbers below are the real measurement with live traces rendering (verified: 2400 points bound on every config).

Short answer: 60/column × 4 is right at the edge (holds ~60 fps but the renderer main thread is ~86 % busy with occasional 33 ms hitches). 80/column × 4 is too much — the main thread saturates (100 %), frame rate drops to ~45 fps, and frames jank to 33–67 ms. For a solid 60 fps with headroom, keep the total on-screen traces ≲ ~160–200.

Setup / method#

  • Where: SpikesPeak live view driven over Chrome DevTools (benchmarks/cdp_bench.py), 1920×1080 window on the primary (landscape) monitor. (Vertical-mode shots are on Monitor 0, the 1080×2560 portrait panel.)
  • Source: simulated NP2 Multishank — one 384-channel RAW band @ 30 kHz, window 1 s. Traces confirmed rendering at every point (bound_pts = 2400).
  • Swept: columns/rows ∈ {1, 2, 4} × channels-per-strip ∈ {20, 40, 60, 80} → on-screen series = columns × channels-per-strip FastLineRenderableSeries (20 → 320). 7 s settle per point.
  • Metrics: fps + frame time (mean/p95/max) from a RAF meter; renderer main-thread busy fraction from CDP Performance.getMetrics; JS heap.
  • Data: benchmarks/bench_channel_density.csv · plot: benchmarks/bench_channel_density.png · screenshots: benchmarks/shots/h_c<cols>_t<traces>.png.

Results (the 4-column case you asked about)#

Layout On-screen traces FPS Frame time (mean / p95 / max) Main-thread busy Verdict
4 × 20 80 60 16.7 / 16.8 / 16.9 ms 40 % ✅ comfortable
4 × 40 160 60 16.7 / 16.8 / 17.3 ms 63 % ✅ fine
4 × 60 240 61 16.7 / 16.8 / 33.4 ms 86 % ⚠️ borderline — occasional hitch
4 × 80 320 45 22.9 / 33.4 / 66.8 ms 100 % ❌ overloaded

Lower column counts have lots of headroom: 1 column to 80 traces = 27 % main-thread @ 60 fps; 2 columns to 160 traces = 45 % @ 60 fps. JS heap stays bounded (20–40 MB, no growth).

Reading it#

  • The cost scales ≈ linearly with total on-screen series (columns × channels-per-strip) — a line series per channel. It crosses from "comfortable" to "saturated" between 240 and 320 traces on this desktop.
  • Main-thread busy is the early-warning metric: once it passes ~85 %, max frame time spikes above 33 ms (a dropped frame); at 100 % the average frame rate itself falls (4×80 → 45 fps).
  • More strips cost a bit more than the same trace count in fewer strips (per-surface axis overhead), so prefer fewer, denser strips — which is exactly what the new vertical (rows) mode gives you.

Recommendation#

  • Everyday: ≤ ~160 on-screen traces (e.g. 4 × 40, or the vertical mode's 4 rows × 40) → rock-solid 60 fps.
  • 60/column × 4 (240): OK to look at, but you're at ~86 % — expect the odd hitch, and no room for more.
  • 80/column × 4 (320): avoid for continuous monitoring — it drops frames.
  • These are best-case (single-band sim on a desktop). Real NP1 dual-band + NI-DAQ + ripple detection + recording add main-thread work on top, so the practical ceiling will be lower — and a laptop lower still. If you need maximum channels on screen, use fewer/denser strips and a modest window (1 s).
