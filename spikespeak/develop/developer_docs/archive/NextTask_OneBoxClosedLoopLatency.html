ARCHIVED — Closed-loop latency on the OneBox, NP1.0 and NP2.0#

DONE 2026-08-20. The full 3x2 matrix ({1 ch, 2 ch quorum 2, 4 ch quorum 3} x {baseline, ASAP}) was completed on both probes, n ≈ 1000 per cell, 0 unmatched. ASAP roughly halves the loop on each (NP1.0 1.44 → 0.92 ms, NP2.0 1.08 → 0.55 ms) and quorum costs nothing; the OneBox matches or beats PXIe in every cell. Results: ../../RecordingTests/CLOSED_LOOP_LATENCY.md; method: ../LatencyProfiling.md; the session write-up is Status §48.

The body below is kept for the operator knowledge in it, not as a plan — how the stim capture moved off the NI-DAQ and onto the OneBox's own ADC, and what has to be true before an ADC-captured pulse can be trusted. Read it before designing the next same-clock measurement.

(Everything from here down is the brief as it was written. Kept unedited except where marked.)

Status: planned, agreed 2026-08-18. Blocked on nothing but the rig — the maintainer streams the synthetic ripple signal when back at work (2026-08-19).

The goal: closed-loop latency recordings and measurements for the ripple → stim path, with the probe on the OneBox rather than the PXIe basestation, for both NP1.0 and NP2.0.

This is the OneBox counterpart of the PXIe latency work already in the log (§27–§30). Everything that matters there — the definition of latency, the analysis tooling, the ASAP delivery flag — carries over unchanged; what is new is the basestation underneath.

Why this is worth doing#

The closed loop is fully quantified on PXIe (§29/§30): sub-millisecond at every quorum level on both probes, NP1.0 0.94–0.98 ms and NP2.0 0.55–0.61 ms with ASAP delivery. Nothing equivalent exists for the OneBox, and the OneBox is a different transport: USB3/FTDI rather than PCIe/MXI. The open question is whether that transport costs latency, and how much.

That is the whole point of the measurement — do not assume it matches PXIe.

Rig wiring as of 2026-08-24 — and why it changed this task#

⚠️ This is a snapshot of the 2026-08-24 bench, and the rig has been rewired twice since. It shows the SMA on IO9, which is no longer where it is. The current authority on which OneBox connector is which — and on the fact that SMA 1 has no IO number because it is patched somewhere different every session — is ../OneBoxWiring.md §4. Keep reading this section for why the IO2 connection mattered; do not wire from its table.

Reported by the maintainer, ahead of the next hardware session:

"npx 2.0 probe has been moved back to the onebox. both systems are powered but no probes on PXIe ... as far as the SDR goes IO6 is connected to IO4 (IO0 to IO11 is the range). SMA on the onebox is connected to IO9 and trigger pin from the pi is connected to IO2"

line connected to what it buys
IO2 Pi stim-server trigger pin The stim pulse is captured on the OneBox's own ADC.
IO9 OneBox SMA the SMA signal becomes readable as an ADC channel
IO6 → IO4 loopback an end-to-end check of the ADC path with a known signal

The IO2 connection is the interesting one. As written below, this task needs the NI-DAQ for the ground-truth capture: the probe stream and the stim pulse have to be sampled on a common clock, and the NI-DAQ was the only thing doing that. With the Pi's trigger on IO2, the OneBox ADC samples the stim pulse itself — on the same device, and the same acquisition, as the probe.

If that holds up, it removes the NI-DAQ from the measurement entirely and with it the sync-alignment step that has already produced one wrong answer (the off-by-one in align_timestamps, §"Sync off-by-one" in the log — a whole-period shift that was invisible because the intervals still looked right). Two streams from one device need no alignment at all.

Not yet verified. Nobody has recorded through this wiring. Before trusting a latency number from it, confirm on the rig that:

  1. the ADC channel for IO2 actually shows the pulse, with an amplitude and width that match what the Pi is asked to emit (the oscilloscope is the reference for width — the OneBox ADC samples every 33 µs and cannot resolve a stim pulse's width; the note is in ../LatencyProfiling.md, in its gotchas section, NOT in Recording.md §5 where this brief first sent people);
  2. the ADC samples and the probe samples carry timestamps from the same clock, rather than two that merely start together;
  3. the IO6 → IO4 loopback reads back what is driven, which is the cheapest way to prove the ADC path before trusting anything measured through it.

Until those three are done, keep the NI-DAQ capture as the reference and treat the ADC capture as the thing being validated, not the thing validating.

Prerequisites#

  • The maintainer streams the synthetic ripple signal into the probe (the same playback used for the PXIe runs). Nothing in this task generates the signal.
  • Stim server reachable — the Pi 3B+ at stimserver@192.168.1.100, UDP 8888 (see raspberrypi_pulsegenerator/). It must be ARMED; the detector arms on connect.
  • NI-DAQ for the ground truth capture: latency is measured offline as ripple threshold-crossing → the stim pulse on ai4, so the NI-DAQ must record alongside the probe. ⚠️ This is the part that needs thought — see "The open question" below.
  • Calibration CSVs for the NP1.0 probe (22175723312) are missing; it currently streams UNCALIBRATED. Worth fixing before taking numbers that will be quoted.

⚠️ The open question to resolve FIRST#

On a PXIe rig the probe and the NI-DAQ share a chassis and a sync line, which is what makes the offline threshold-crossing → ai4 measurement meaningful. With the probe on the OneBox, work out how the NI-DAQ capture is synchronised to it before recording anything, because the whole measurement rests on that alignment:

  • Does the existing NPX↔NI-DAQ sync-pulse alignment still hold when the probe is on USB?
  • Or should the OneBox's own 12-channel ADC capture the stim pulse instead of the NI-DAQ, making the measurement single-device and removing the cross-device sync entirely? The ADC is verified working (0.000152588 V/count, §35.4) and records to data.npx.adc with a well-formed sidecar.

The second option is the more attractive one and is probably the right answer — it collapses a two-clock problem into one clock. Decide deliberately; do not default to copying the PXIe recipe.

Remember the sync off-by-one that cost 30+ minutes previously (§24): verify closed-loop timing event-by-event, never by nearest-neighbour matching on a periodic signal.

Method (mirrors the PXIe runs)#

  1. Bring up Npx1OneBox / Npx2OneBox, confirm the detector fires on the synthetic signal.
  2. Record with the detector's resolved read-out channels plus the stim capture channel.
  3. Run the existing analysis: ripple_tools/analyze_recording.py, latency_breakdown.py, verify_quorum.py. sync.py defaults to match="auto".
  4. Repeat the quorum × delivery matrix if time allows; at minimum ASAP vs baseline on both probes.
  5. Compare against the PXIe numbers and state plainly whether USB3 costs latency.
