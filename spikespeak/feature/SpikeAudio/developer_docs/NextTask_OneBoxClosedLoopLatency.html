NEXT TASK — Closed-loop latency on the OneBox, NP1.0 and NP2.0#

Status: planned, agreed 2026-08-18. Blocked on nothing but the rig — the maintainer streams the synthetic ripple signal when back at work (2026-08-19).

The goal: closed-loop latency recordings and measurements for the ripple → stim path, with the probe on the OneBox rather than the PXIe basestation, for both NP1.0 and NP2.0.

This is the OneBox counterpart of the PXIe latency work already in the log (§27–§30). Everything that matters there — the definition of latency, the analysis tooling, the ASAP delivery flag — carries over unchanged; what is new is the basestation underneath.

Why this is worth doing#

The closed loop is fully quantified on PXIe (§29/§30): sub-millisecond at every quorum level on both probes, NP1.0 0.94–0.98 ms and NP2.0 0.55–0.61 ms with ASAP delivery. Nothing equivalent exists for the OneBox, and the OneBox is a different transport: USB3/FTDI rather than PCIe/MXI. The open question is whether that transport costs latency, and how much.

That is the whole point of the measurement — do not assume it matches PXIe.

Prerequisites#

  • The maintainer streams the synthetic ripple signal into the probe (the same playback used for the PXIe runs). Nothing in this task generates the signal.
  • Stim server reachable — the Pi 3B+ at stimserver@192.168.1.100, UDP 8888 (see raspberrypi_pulsegenerator/). It must be ARMED; the detector arms on connect.
  • NI-DAQ for the ground truth capture: latency is measured offline as ripple threshold-crossing → the stim pulse on ai4, so the NI-DAQ must record alongside the probe. ⚠️ This is the part that needs thought — see "The open question" below.
  • Calibration CSVs for the NP1.0 probe (22175723312) are missing; it currently streams UNCALIBRATED. Worth fixing before taking numbers that will be quoted.

⚠️ The open question to resolve FIRST#

On a PXIe rig the probe and the NI-DAQ share a chassis and a sync line, which is what makes the offline threshold-crossing → ai4 measurement meaningful. With the probe on the OneBox, work out how the NI-DAQ capture is synchronised to it before recording anything, because the whole measurement rests on that alignment:

  • Does the existing NPX↔NI-DAQ sync-pulse alignment still hold when the probe is on USB?
  • Or should the OneBox's own 12-channel ADC capture the stim pulse instead of the NI-DAQ, making the measurement single-device and removing the cross-device sync entirely? The ADC is verified working (0.000152588 V/count, §35.4) and records to data.npx.adc with a well-formed sidecar.

The second option is the more attractive one and is probably the right answer — it collapses a two-clock problem into one clock. Decide deliberately; do not default to copying the PXIe recipe.

Remember the sync off-by-one that cost 30+ minutes previously (§24): verify closed-loop timing event-by-event, never by nearest-neighbour matching on a periodic signal.

Method (mirrors the PXIe runs)#

  1. Bring up Npx1OneBox / Npx2OneBox, confirm the detector fires on the synthetic signal.
  2. Record with the detector's resolved read-out channels plus the stim capture channel.
  3. Run the existing analysis: ripple_tools/analyze_recording.py, latency_breakdown.py, verify_quorum.py. sync.py defaults to match="auto".
  4. Repeat the quorum × delivery matrix if time allows; at minimum ASAP vs baseline on both probes.
  5. Compare against the PXIe numbers and state plainly whether USB3 costs latency.

Deliverables#

  • Recordings under RecordingTests/<date>_onebox_closed_loop/, raw .bin not committed (gitignored); derived figures + metrics in that session's analysis/.
  • A latency table for NP1.0 and NP2.0 on the OneBox, directly comparable to the PXIe matrix in §29/§30.
  • A new dated section in SpikesPeak_Status.md.
