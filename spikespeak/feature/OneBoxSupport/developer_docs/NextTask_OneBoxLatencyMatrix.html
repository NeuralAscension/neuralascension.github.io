Analysis, per cell#

python ripple_tools/analyze_recording.py <dir>/<name> --name <name> \
  --stim-band adc --stim-channel 2 \
  --ws <dir>/<name>_ws.jsonl --ripple-sidecar <dir>/<name>_ripple.json \
  --figs <dir>/analysis

Two things this line gets right that an earlier version of it did not, both of which cost a full re-run to discover:

  • recdir is POSITIONAL. There is no --dir. The earlier form failed instantly with unrecognized arguments: --dir on every cell.
  • It is the INNER directory. record_ripples.py --dir D --name N writes the .bin and .bin.json into D/N/, and leaves N_ws.jsonl and N_ripple.json in D/ beside it. Passing D gives No SpikesPeak recordings (*.bin.json) found.

And do not pipe the command into tail: a shell pipeline exits with the status of its LAST member, so a failing analysis reports success. That is how five cells were "analysed" without producing a single number.

--stim-band adc --stim-channel 2 is not optional on a OneBox. Without it the analyser looks for a nidaq band, finds none, and silently reports no closed-loop latency at all. Channel 2 is the Pi's On/Off line (GPIO17 = header pin 11) into OneBox IO2.

What was fixed before this run, and why it matters#

A first attempt at cell 1 reached 944 detections with ZERO stim fires, which would have been a 634 MB recording that looks complete and cannot yield a latency.

The cause: the gate and the arm are separate, and both are required. set_stim_enabled only permits datagrams to leave the host; the stimulator must also be armed before a detection triggers it. The backend's auto-arm on source selection was deliberately removed (selecting any source — even one whose connect then failed — put a datagram on the wire), and nothing replaced it for scripted runs. The GUI arms via its stim LED; record_ripples.py had no equivalent, so it enabled the gate, detected happily, and fired nothing. The log said so plainly and nobody was reading it:

[RippleDetector] stimulation ENABLED -> "192.168.1.100:8888" (not armed; arm from the GUI stim LED)

Two changes:

  • Rig.arm_stim() added, and record_ripples.py now arms after enabling the gate, then prints the stim status so the arming is visible at the top of the run.
  • The end-of-run summary warns loudly if stimulation was requested and nothing fired, because a pulse-less closed-loop recording is otherwise indistinguishable from a good one until analysis.

Two operational notes#

  • Run each cell in the background, or with a long timeout. A cell takes roughly 10 minutes (~500 ms between detections at 1000 detections). A 10-minute foreground limit killed the first attempt at 944/1000. The hardware was fine — the teardown still ran and the OneBox closed properly — but the run was lost.
  • Disk: each cell is ~600–700 MB, so the five are ~3.5 GB. There was 330 GB free at the time of writing. Recording data stays local; it is never committed.
